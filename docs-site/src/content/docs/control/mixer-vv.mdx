This plan takes [`Mixer`](/control/mixer/) to grade **A** under the
[building block standard](/standards/building-blocks/). It lists where the
mixer stands against each requirement, the animations that illustrate its
checks, the evidence ledger, and a sequence of PRs.

**Scope.** The mixer is an algebraic map with no state: an exact linear
allocation, then a saturation priority. Its specification is the
allocation and the priority's postconditions, so there is no continuous
model to converge to. Verification checks that it computes the inverse
exactly, that its output meets the postconditions everywhere in the demand
space, and that its priority behaves as specified in closed loop. Whether
the priority suits a vehicle's flying is the business of the loops around
it, the [cascade](/control/cascade/) above all.

**Consumers.** The cascade's output stage in racing-drone's B0 baseline:
`mix()` once per rate tick at 1 kHz, its report fed back to the rate and
velocity loops, and `to_rotor_command()` to the ESCs.

## Animations

These animations illustrate three of the requirements above. Each plays a
trace from a scenario in `tools/trace/scenarios/mixer/`, and each scenario
computes the check shown under its player. They are not the evidence for
the requirement (ANI-4): the tests are, and the status table follows them.

### The priority's postcondition (BB-T1)

`mix()` promises that the achieved torque is the demand scaled by σ for
roll and pitch and by s for yaw: the direction of roll and pitch is kept,
whatever is cut. The scenario checks it at every sample of a demand that
crosses all three stages of the priority.

### Continuity across the boundaries (BB-T1)

The unit test `the output is continuous across the saturation boundary`
walks the collective through the point where the top rotor reaches T_max,
and requires no thrust to step by more than the demand does. The scenario
sweeps the whole throttle range, through all four boundaries.
