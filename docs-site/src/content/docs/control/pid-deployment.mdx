This guide is for integrators who build a loop from [`Pid`](/control/pid/),
or who tune the loops the [cascade](/control/cascade/) builds from it. It
covers what to set, how to wire the controller into a loop, and what it
does at the edges. The [developer guide](/control/pid/) has the control law
and the API.

## Configuration

`PidConfig<Real>` is a plain aggregate. `pid_config_valid()` (I1) must hold
before a controller is built: non-negative gains, `min <= max` for both
limit pairs, and `derivative_tau >= 0`. The output's unit is the loop's
choice. The gains take whatever units make $K_p e$, $K_i \int e\,dt$, and
$K_d \dot y$ come out in it.

| Field | Unit | Default | What it sets |
|---|---|---|---|
| `kp` | output / error | 0 | Proportional gain |
| `ki` | output / (error·s) | 0 | Integral gain |
| `kd` | output·s / error | 0 | Derivative gain, on the measurement |
| `output_min`, `output_max` | output | −1, 1 | Output saturation, and the trigger for anti-windup |
| `integral_min`, `integral_max` | output | −1, 1 | Bounds on the integral term itself |
| `derivative_tau` | s | 0 | Time constant of the low-pass on the measured rate; 0 is no filter |

The defaults describe a normalized command with no gains. They are not a
tuning. The cascade derives its gains from the vehicle in
`cascade_config_from()`; for RP-1 they are:

| Loop | k_p | k_i | k_d | Output limit | Integral limit | τ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate, roll and pitch (rad/s → rad/s²) | 50 /s | 250 /s² | 0.4 | ±622 rad/s² | ±156 rad/s² | 2 ms |
| Rate, yaw | 50 /s | 250 /s² | 0.4 | ±76.8 rad/s² | ±19.2 rad/s² | 2 ms |
| Velocity, each axis (m/s → m/s²) | 3.57 /s | 1.28 /s² | 0 | ±9.81 m/s² | ±4.90 m/s² | 0 |

The rate loop's output limit is the vehicle's angular acceleration at full
differential thrust, and its integral may use a quarter of that. The
velocity loop's output limit is one g.

## Choosing the limits

**Output limits** are the actuator's authority, in the output's unit. Set
them to what the stage after the controller can deliver. Anti-windup
engages at these limits, so limits wider than the actuator turn it off: the
[explainer](/control/pid-explainer/#5-saturation-windup-and-anti-windup)
shows what that costs.

**Integral limits** bound the disturbance the loop can cancel without a
steady error. At rest, the integral supplies whatever the plant needs that
the setpoint does not: a heavier vehicle, a trim offset, a steady wind. A
narrow range keeps a wound-up integral from taking over the loop. Too
narrow, and the loop settles with an error.

Size the integral range for the largest steady disturbance you expect, with
margin, and no wider. The cascade gives its rate loop a quarter of the
output range, and its velocity loop half a g.

## External saturation

A stage after the controller can deliver less than the controller asked
for: the [mixer](/control/mixer/#saturation-priority) when the rotors run out
of authority, or a thrust limiter. The controller's own limits do not see
that cut, so without help its integral winds up behind it. The
four-argument `update(setpoint, measurement, dt, external_saturation)`
takes the direction of the last cut, +1 when the stage delivered less than
the output, −1 when it delivered more, and 0 when it did not cut. The
integral then freezes by the same rule as at the controller's own limits.

```cpp
int cut = 0;  // the stage's report from the previous tick
for (;;) {
    const double u = pid.update(setpoint, measurement, dt, cut);
    const double delivered = stage.apply(u);
    cut = delivered < u ? 1 : (delivered > u ? -1 : 0);
}
```

The cascade does exactly this with the mixer's report, through
`Cascade::feedback()`.

## The derivative filter and the loop rate

The filter is a first-order low-pass on the measured rate, with corner
frequency $1/(2\pi\tau)$: 80 Hz at the cascade's τ = 2 ms. Two bounds apply.

- **Below:** τ of at least a loop period or two, or the filter passes the
  sample-to-sample noise through. The cascade's 2 ms is two periods of its
  1 kHz rate loop.
- **Above:** τ small against the loop's own time scale, or the damping
  arrives late and the loop overshoots. The
  [explainer's widget](/control/pid-explainer/#4-filter-the-derivative)
  shows the overshoot doubling once τ reaches a few times that scale.

`derivative_tau = 0` takes the raw finite difference, which is right only
for a clean measurement or no D term.

## Timing

- **Pass the measured dt.** `update()` integrates and differentiates with
  the `dt` it is given, so pass the time since the last call, not the
  nominal period.
- **A non-positive dt is a fault.** `update()` returns the last output and
  leaves its state alone. A duplicated tick or a clock that steps back is
  then harmless.
- **A long dt is accepted.** After a missed deadline, the integral takes the
  whole gap in one step. The controller cannot tell a late tick from a slow
  loop, so detect overruns in the scheduler. The
  [developer guide](/control/pid/#reading-a-timing-fault-in-a-trace) replays
  each case.
- **Cost.** About 12–13 ns per update on x86, for float and double alike
  (`bench/pid_bench`, GCC 15.2 Release, i7-12700K). The operation count is
  fixed for a given configuration; measure on the flight processor before
  budgeting.

## Lifecycle

- **Gains are fixed at construction.** There is no setter. To retune, build
  a new controller and go through the lifecycle
  (`Unconfigured → Inactive → Active`).
- **Reset on arming and on a mode switch.** `reset()` zeroes the integral
  and the rate, and unprimes the derivative, so the first update after it
  has no derivative term.
- **No bumpless transfer.** After `reset()`, the output starts from the P
  term and a zero integral. A loop taking over from another, at a nonzero
  command, steps at the switch. Pid has no way to preload its integral.

## Scalar types

Pid uses only `+ - * /`, `<`, and construction from an integer. The test
suite runs it in double, float, `posit<32,2>`, `posit<16,1>`, and
`cfloat<32,8>`. In closed loop the 32-bit types stay within 1e-5 of double,
and `posit<16,1>` within 1e-2, on a loop with outputs of order 1. Scale the
loop so that its signals sit near 1 when you use a 16-bit type.

## Consumer contract

| | |
|---|---|
| Preconditions | `pid_config_valid(cfg)` at construction; finite inputs |
| Postconditions | The output is within `[output_min, output_max]`, and the integral within its bounds, after every call |
| Faults | dt ≤ 0 returns the last output with the state unchanged |
| State | The integral, the filtered rate, the last measurement and output, and whether it is primed; `reset()` returns to the constructed state |
| Resources | `noexcept`; no allocation or system calls; constant time; usable in `constexpr` |
| Not provided | Setpoint weighting, feed-forward, gain scheduling, bumpless transfer, dt validation beyond the sign |
