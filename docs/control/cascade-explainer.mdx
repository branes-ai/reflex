A quadrotor's rotors all push along one axis, the body's z axis. To move
sideways the vehicle has to tilt that axis, to tilt it has to turn, and to
turn it needs a torque, which takes the motors a moment to deliver. So
[`Cascade`](cascade.mdx) is four loops, nested, each asking the one
inside it for what it needs:

```
position ──▶ velocity ──(tilt, thrust)──▶ attitude ──(body rates)──▶ rate ──(torque)──▶ mixer
```

Each loop can only be as fast as the one inside it lets it be, so each one
is tuned slower than the next. This page shows the loops at work, from the
inside out.

Every animation plays a trace recorded from the cascade's own C++, flying
the [quadrotor model](../model/quadrotor.mdx) through the [mixer](mixer.mdx),
and the scenario is linked under each player. The two widgets with sliders
rerun that C++, compiled to WebAssembly, in your browser. The vehicle is
RP-1: 1 kg, motors with a 20 ms lag, and at most 48 N of thrust. The
controller sees the true state, with no sensor noise or delay. The controls
and keys are described on the
[Trace Player](../standards/trace-player.mdx#player-behaviour) page.

## The four loops

| Loop | Input | Output | Runs at | Speed of response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate | body-rate setpoint ω_sp | torque, through an angular acceleration | 1 kHz | 50 /s |
| Attitude | attitude target q_sp | ω_sp | 250 Hz | 10 /s for tilt, 5 /s for heading |
| Velocity | velocity setpoint v_sp | a tilt and a collective thrust | 50 Hz | 3.6 /s |
| Position | position setpoint | v_sp | 50 Hz | 1.6 /s |

The speeds come from the motors outwards. The motor lag τ = 20 ms sets the
rate loop at 1/τ = 50 /s, and each loop outside it is several times slower:
the attitude loop by 5, the velocity loop by 14, the position loop by 32.
[`cascade_config_from()`](cascade.mdx#default-gains) derives every
gain this way from the vehicle.

## 1. Three loops, three rates

The inner loops run more often than the outer ones, because they have more
to do in less time. On every rate-loop tick (1 ms), `update()` runs the rate
loop. On every 4th it runs the attitude loop first, and on every 20th the
position loop before that. Between its runs each outer loop holds its last
output.

Holding an output costs nothing, so running the outer loops slower spends
the processor on the loop that needs it. It also gives each inner loop a
steady setpoint to settle on before the next one arrives.

## 2. The inner loops: a roll step

Start with the inner two loops alone, in attitude mode: the caller sets
the attitude and the thrust, and the position loop does not run. The
attitude loop turns the angle error into a roll-rate setpoint, K times the
error. The rate loop turns the rate error into an angular acceleration,
k_p times the error plus damping, and multiplies it by the inertia to get a
torque.

*Before you play:* the attitude loop runs at K = 10 /s, a fifth of the rate
loop's 50 /s. What happens to the roll when the two come close?

When the two loops' speeds come close, the attitude loop asks for rates
faster than the rate loop can deliver, and the roll overshoots. The default
ratio of 5 leaves margin for slower motors, more inertia, and delay. The
closed-loop tests require at most 25 % overshoot with the motors twice as
slow, 30 % more inertia, and 4 ms of delay (the
[V&V plan](cascade-vv.mdx#where-it-stands) has the full set).

The rate loop works in angular acceleration, not torque. Its gains have
units of 1/s, whatever the vehicle weighs, and the inertia turns its output
into a torque. A heavier vehicle with the same motors keeps the same gains.

## 3. Tilt first, then heading

The tilt points the thrust, so it decides where the vehicle goes. The
heading does not move the vehicle at all. The attitude loop therefore
splits each attitude error in two: the tilt that moves the thrust axis, and
then a turn about it. It runs the tilt at 10 /s and the turn at 5 /s, capped
at 4 rad/s, and neither part slows the other.

*Before you play:* two vehicles are given the same 30° roll. One of them
(solid) is also asked to turn 90° about its thrust axis. Which one gets its
tilt right first?

The roll-rate setpoint jumps to 5.2 rad/s at the step, 10 /s times the
30° (0.52 rad) error. The yaw-rate setpoint sits at its limit, 4 rad/s. A
controller that worked on Euler angles would mix the two: the turn would
change the roll and pitch errors as it went, and the tilt would wander.
Here the tilt error depends on the tilt alone, so it falls exactly as it
does without the turn.

## 4. Flying a path: the waypoint square

In position mode all four loops run. The position loop turns the position
error into a velocity setpoint, and the velocity loop turns the velocity
error into an acceleration. To make an acceleration, the vehicle tilts its
thrust toward it: the velocity loop hands the attitude loop a tilt and a
collective thrust.

A loop that only reacts to errors needs an error before it does anything.
So a setpoint can also carry the velocity and acceleration the path calls
for, as feed-forward: the position loop adds the velocity to its output,
and the velocity loop adds the acceleration. The loops then have only the
difference to correct.

*Before you play:* the setpoint goes around a 10 m square at 5 m/s at
mid-leg, stopping at each corner, with full feed-forward. The translucent
vehicle is where the position loop wants the vehicle to be, with the
attitude it asks for. How far behind it does the vehicle fall?

Without feed-forward the position loop is the only thing that moves the
vehicle, and it moves it only once it has fallen behind. At 5 m/s the
vehicle trails the setpoint by up to 2.7 m and reaches only 4.5 m/s, just
inside the criterion's 10 %. It still stays on the square, within 0.09 m,
because each leg is straight and ends at rest. Feed-forward fixes the
timing, not the path.

At 8 m/s with full feed-forward the profile itself asks for 6.4 m/s², a 33°
tilt, and the corrections on top reach the 40° tilt limit. The vehicle then
cannot accelerate as asked. It runs 0.61 m wide of the corners, past the
criterion's 0.6 m, and the check fails, though the RMS error is still
0.22 m.

## 5. Running out of thrust: a capped climb

Each loop has limits. The velocity setpoint is capped (8 m/s horizontal,
3 m/s up, 2 m/s down), the tilt at 40°, and the collective between 10 % of
hover and 80 % of the rotors' full thrust. When a limit cuts, the loop
inside cannot deliver what the loop outside asks. An integrator that kept
integrating the error would wind up, and the vehicle would overshoot once
the limit let go. So the position loop reports each cut, and on the next
tick the velocity PID on that axis stops integrating in the direction of
the cut. The mixer's saturation report does the same for the rate loop
([anti-windup](cascade.mdx#anti-windup)).

*Before you play:* the collective is capped at 1.25 times the weight, and
the setpoint jumps 10 m up. The velocity loop asks for twice the weight.
What happens to the vertical integrator while the cap holds, and does the
vehicle overshoot 15 m?
