This guide is for integrators who put [`Mixer`](mixer.mdx) between
the control loops and the motors. It covers what to set, what the vehicle
can then do, and how to wire the mixer into a loop. The
[developer guide](mixer.mdx) has the allocation, the priority, and
the API.

## Parameters

`MixerParams<T>` is a plain aggregate. `mixer_params_valid()` (I1) must hold
before a mixer is built: every value finite, L, k_m, and k_f positive, and
0 ≤ T_min < T_max.

| Field | Unit | RP-1 | What it sets |
|---|---|---|---|
| `arm_length` | m | 0.11 | L, centre to rotor axis; the roll and pitch arm is a = L/√2 |
| `torque_coefficient` | m | 0.016 | k_m: a rotor's drag torque per newton of thrust |
| `thrust_coefficient` | N/(rad/s)² | 8.9e-7 | k_f in T = k_f·ω², for `to_rotor_command()` |
| `min_thrust` | N | 0 | T_min per rotor |
| `max_thrust` | N | 12 | T_max per rotor |

`mixer_params_from(vehicle)` copies L, k_m, k_f, and T_max from the
`QuadrotorParams` the model flies, and sets T_min = 0. Measure k_m and k_f
on a thrust stand for your motor, propeller, and battery: k_m sets how much
thrust difference a yaw torque costs, and k_f how fast the rotors spin for
a thrust.

## What the vehicle can do

The parameters fix the wrench the rotors can make. For RP-1:

| Demand alone | Limit | Needs a collective of |
|---|---|---|
| Collective thrust | 4·T_max = 48 N, 4.9 g for 1 kg | — |
| Roll or pitch torque | 2a·(T_max − T_min) = 1.87 N·m, 622 rad/s² | 2·(T_max + T_min) = 24 N |
| Roll and pitch together, diagonally | 0.93 N·m on each axis, 1.32 N·m in all | 24 N |
| Yaw torque | 2k_m·(T_max − T_min) = 0.38 N·m, 77 rad/s² | 24 N |
| Yaw torque at hover (9.81 N) | 4k_m·(F/4 − T_min) = 0.16 N·m | — |

A diagonal tilt has less authority because it uses only one diagonal pair
of rotors. Yaw has the least: k_m is a fifth of the moment arm. The
cascade's rate-loop output limits, 622 rad/s² for roll and pitch and
76.8 rad/s² for yaw, are these limits divided by the inertia.

Away from a collective of 24 N, the room shrinks. Near full throttle the
mixer lowers the collective to keep the tilt, and near zero it raises it.

## Choosing the minimum thrust

T_min is the least thrust the mixer will command. With T_min = 0 the mixer
may stop a rotor in flight, when the tilt needs it at the bottom of the
throttle. Many ESCs and motors cannot restart a stopped rotor quickly, and
some lose sync. Set T_min to the thrust at the motor's reliable idle speed,
measured on the stand, when that matters for your hardware.

T_min costs authority: every limit in the table above scales with
T_max − T_min. With T_min = 0.5 N, roll and pitch get 1.79 N·m rather than
1.87 N·m. Airmode keeps working: the collective rises until the lowest
rotor sits at T_min.

## Arming, idle, and the ground

The mixer raises the collective whenever roll or pitch need it, even with
the throttle at zero (airmode). So it will spin the rotors up on the ground
if the loops ask for torque, and on the ground the loops' integrators wind
up against the ground's reaction. The mixer has no notion of armed,
landed, or idle: the state machine around it does.

- **Disarmed:** do not call `mix()`. Command the motors off directly.
- **Armed on the ground:** command idle directly, or hold the loops'
  integrators reset and their outputs at zero until take-off.
- **In flight:** call `mix()` every rate tick.

## Wiring it into a loop

Once per rate tick, in this order:

```cpp
const control::Wrench<double> w = cascade.update(state, setpoint, dt);
const control::MixerOutput<double> out = mixer.mix(w);
cascade.feedback(out);  // the integrators see this tick's cuts next tick
const model::RotorCommand<double> u = mixer.to_rotor_command(out.thrusts);
esc.write(u);           // in Quad X order: 0 FR, 1 RL, 2 FL, 3 RR
```

- **Report the cuts.** `feedback()` keeps `saturation_from(w, out)`, and on
  the next tick the cascade's PIDs freeze their integrals on the axes the
  mixer cut (see [anti-windup](cascade.mdx#anti-windup)). Without it their integrators wind up
  against the mixer while it cuts, and the vehicle overshoots once the cut
  ends (the PID's
  [external saturation](pid-deployment.mdx#external-saturation)).
  A loop of your own should do the same with the report.
- **Rotor order.** The thrusts come out in PX4's Quad X order with PX4's
  spin directions. If your frame's motor outputs are numbered differently,
  permute after `to_rotor_command()`, and check each motor's spin direction
  against the table in the [developer guide](mixer.mdx#allocation):
  a reversed motor turns a yaw command into its opposite.
- **Only Quad X.** Other frames (H, plus, hexacopters) need a different
  allocation; this mixer does not take one.

## Rotor commands and the battery

`to_rotor_command()` inverts T = k_f·ω² with k_f for a full battery. As the
battery sags, the same speed command gives less thrust, and the loops make
up the difference through their errors and integrals. What the mixer
reports is a cut of the *commanded* thrust. A rotor that cannot reach its
command, because of a sagging battery or the motor's lag (τ = 20 ms on
RP-1), is not in the report. The loops see that only through the vehicle's
response.

## Cost

About 19 ns per `mix()` call and 3 ns per `allocate()` on x86, for float
and double alike (`bench/mixer_bench`, GCC 15.2 Release, i7-12700K; 15 to
17 ns for `mix()` with Clang 21), over demands that sweep the throttle range
and take every stage of the priority.
The operation count is bounded, with no calls
(`tests/sdk/mixer_resource.cpp`): `mix()` runs 47 arithmetic operations,
one more when roll and pitch scale and one more for a yaw demand, and two
more for each time rounding leaves one of its intervals empty, at most 53.
`allocate()` runs 14, and `to_rotor_command()` 4 divisions and four square
roots. Measure on the flight processor before budgeting.

## Scalar types

The tests run in double and float, with tolerances by precision. The mixer
uses `+ − × ÷`, comparisons, `abs`, `isfinite`, and, in `to_rotor_command()`
and the constructor, `sqrt`.

## Consumer contract

| | |
|---|---|
| Preconditions | `mixer_params_valid(p)` at construction; a finite wrench; finite, non-negative thrusts for `to_rotor_command()` |
| Postconditions | Every thrust within [T_min, T_max]; the achieved torque is (στx, στy, sτz) with σ ∈ (0, 1], s ∈ [0, 1]; a demand that fits comes back unchanged |
| Faults | None reported: a precondition violation is an `assert` in Debug builds |
| State | None: the mixer holds only its parameters and values derived from them |
| Resources | No allocation; at most 53 arithmetic operations per `mix()`, with no calls; `saturation_from()` is `noexcept` |
| Not provided | Arming and idle, frames other than Quad X, battery compensation, motor-lag compensation, a configurable priority |
