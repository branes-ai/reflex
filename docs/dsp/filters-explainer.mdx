The rate loop flies the vehicle from the gyro, and the gyro is bolted to a
frame that four spinning rotors shake. It measures the vehicle's turning
and the vibration together. The vibration is loud and narrow: it sits at
each rotor's rotation frequency and at whole multiples of it, the
harmonics. If it reaches the rate loop, the loop treats the vibration as
turning and drives the motors against it. The motors run hot, the
vibration gets worse, and in the worst case the loop chases it into an
oscillation.

[The filters](filters.md) take the vibration out before the rate loop sees
it. This page builds up how, in four steps:
1. one notch;
2. notches that follow the rotors;
3. what happens at low throttle;
4. what all filtering costs.

Every animation plays a trace recorded from the filters' own C++, and the
scenario is linked under each player. Its spectrograms are computed in
that C++ too. The throttle-sweep widget reruns that C++, compiled to
WebAssembly, in your browser. The controls and keys are described on the
[Trace Player](../standards/trace-player.mdx#player-behaviour) page.

## Where the vibration sits

| RP-1 rotors | Rotor speed | Fundamental | 2nd harmonic | 3rd harmonic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Idle (in these scenarios) | 440 rad/s | 70 Hz | 140 Hz | 210 Hz |
| Hover | 1660 rad/s | 264 Hz | 528 Hz | 792 Hz |
| Maximum | 3672 rad/s | 584 Hz | 1168 Hz | 1753 Hz |

The vehicle's own motion is slow by comparison: the rate loop's crossover
is at 6.3 Hz. Between the two lies a gap of more than a decade, and the
filters work in that gap. The scenarios' gyro signal has three parts:
- a 5 Hz roll rate of 0.5 rad/s;
- the first three harmonics of four rotors spread ±5 % around one
  another, 0.1, 0.05, and 0.03 rad/s at hover and growing with the square
  of the speed;
- 0.002 rad/s of sensor noise.

These amplitudes are illustrative; flight logs set them for a real
vehicle.

A spectrogram shows the signal as frequency against time: each column is
the spectrum of the last 32 ms, and the colour is the level in dB, from
−60 dB (pale) to 0 dB (dark). A steady tone is a horizontal line, and a
rotor speeding up is a line that climbs. The part after the cursor is
dimmed, so the spectrogram fills in as the trace plays.

## 1. A notch removes one frequency

A notch is a filter with a gain of exactly zero at one frequency, f₀, and
close to one everywhere else. How wide the dip is depends on the quality
factor q: the band where the gain is below −3 dB (half the power) is about
f₀/q wide. Here f₀ is RP-1's hover fundamental, 264 Hz, and q = 5.

*Before you play:* a tone sweeps from 50 Hz to 650 Hz through the notch.
Over what range of frequencies does the notch take the tone away?

The −3 dB band is 52 Hz wide, as the closed form for this design predicts,
(f_s/π)·atan(sin ω₀ / 2q) = 52.5 Hz. The dip is narrow: at 6.3 Hz, where
the rate loop works, the notch's gain is 1 to within 0.01 %. A notch costs
the rate loop little, as long as it sits where the vibration is.

## 2. Notches that follow the rotors

A fixed notch only helps while the rotor runs at the speed it was set for.
The rotors change speed with every throttle movement and every correction,
so `RpmNotch` moves its notches with them. Each tick it is given the four
rotor speeds, from the ESC telemetry on hardware or the model in
simulation. It places a notch at each of the first three harmonics of each
rotor, twelve in all, and runs the gyro through all of them in series.

*Before you play:* the throttle sweeps from idle to 90 % of the rotors'
maximum speed. The top spectrogram is the raw gyro. In the bottom one,
filtered by the tracked notches, the orange lines are the notch centres.
What is left of the vibration, and where?

The tracked notches stay on the harmonics as the rotors speed up, and the
vibration they leave is 40 to 80 dB down: a hundredth to a ten-thousandth
of its amplitude. While the speeds change, each notch trails its harmonic
slightly; once they hold still, the notches settle and the vibration left
falls below −100 dB. A bank of notches fixed at hover does well only while
the rotors are near hover. Below and above it, it misses the lines and
leaves −10 dB or more.

The notches can only be as good as the speeds they are given. With a 2 %
speed error, the q = 5 notches sit a tenth of their −3 dB width off the
harmonics, and the vibration left rises from −71 dB to −32 dB, just
inside the −30 dB criterion. At 5 % it is −19 dB, and the criterion fails.
Wider notches tolerate more: at q = 2 a 5 % error still leaves only
−44 dB, at a cost that [section 4](#4-the-price-delay) shows.

## 3. Staying out of the control band

In the first half second the fundamentals, at 66 to 74 Hz, pass through
the filtered spectrogram untouched. That is deliberate. A notch removes a
band, not a line, and a notch at 70 Hz with q = 5 would cut into the
frequencies the rate loop works at, and add phase lag there. So
`RpmNotch` has a floor, `min_hz` = 100 Hz by default. A notch whose
harmonic is below it sits at `min_hz` with weight 0, and the stage passes
its input through unchanged. Between `min_hz` and `min_hz + fade_hz`
(150 Hz) the notch fades in linearly, so that it does not switch on with a
jump.

Each notch's output is blended as y ← y + w·(N(y) − y), with weight w
from 0 to 1. Every notch keeps filtering even at w = 0, so its history is
current when it fades back in. The second and third harmonics are above
100 Hz from the start, so their notches are on through the idle.

## 4. The price: delay

A filter cannot remove a frequency without delaying the frequencies it
keeps, and delay is what the rate loop can least afford: it costs phase
margin, the loop's distance from oscillating. So the gyro chain is part of
the rate loop's design. Here it is the chain of the
[delay-budget test](filters-vv.mdx#acceptance-criteria):
- a 2nd-order Butterworth low-pass at 101 Hz, 16 times the crossover, for
  the broadband noise;
- the twelve RPM notches at the hover speeds.

*Before you play:* a 1 rad/s roll-rate step reaches the gyro at 5 ms. How
long does each filter take to pass on half of it?

At the crossover, 6.3 Hz, the chain lags by 7.1°: 5.1° from the low-pass
and 2.0° from the twelve notches. The rate loop's phase margin falls from
60.1° to 53.0°, inside the 45° budget with 8° to spare. That spare is what
a deployment spends on the rest of its delay: the IMU, the ESC, and the
loop's own scheduling. Widening the notches to q = 2 costs 5.0° instead
of 2.0°. The [deployment guide](filters-deployment.mdx#choosing-q) works
through the trade.
